My journey with the world of computing is one of enchantment and disenchantment. The discovery of a world that opened up with the first programmed algorithms: the democratization of a tool capable of improving health, sport, and work. And the grief of seeing that same tool used to spread hatred, addiction, and alienation. And, in this journey where we see technology growing ever closer to being a life partner, here I want to keep a little primer of what makes us most unique in this relationship between person and machine. Emotion.

When I was deep in studies on generative AI and coming to understand the relationships between words and concepts, I realized there's a certain predictability in how these two things are presented to us. After all, with an enormous amount of data and well-calibrated weight systems, an AI model is a powerful tool for calculating the next word.

When we read a text, our brain absorbs it word by word, searching for a logical reason behind what's being presented. And so we end up in a kind of game where the expectation for the next word needs to be satisfied.

So, can an AI create any text?

To answer that, let's expand a bit more on the concept of artificial intelligence.

I like Jaron Lanier's definition:

"AI is a network of artifacts created by humanity, organized into a system of weights that can generate new artifacts from what it has stored."

It's an excellent tool for generating things that follow already well-defined patterns and rules. The models in use already come with a series of predetermined definitions that, for the most part, deliver satisfactory results. The downside is that a simple prompt like "generate a promotional card" produces a feed that's completely repetitive and monotonous — after all, the underlying data and weight models come straight off the factory floor.

But where do we fit into that process?

To dig into this, I found myself asking what makes emotional triggers fire in people as they go through a text — and even how a comedian makes me laugh, or why I cry when someone tells me something sad.

And from what I understood, it's exactly the breaking of the expectation for the next word.

When the construction of a sentence or an idea is, in some way, surprising, we react by trying to build a logic around what we've heard, drawing on a much richer set of tools than words to connect with the other person.

We laugh, we cry. And the other person understands.

Emotions are the greatest source for marking identity. It's through them that, when we encounter someone else, we discover what we most want to receive and, more importantly, what we most want to convey.

I'd bet that's exactly what will set us apart going forward.

If AI keeps evolving to handle the monotonous, predictable parts of our lives, then it's up to us to return to our communities and discover what makes us unique among people.

Doing what we do best.

Breaking expectations, and letting the emotions go through.
